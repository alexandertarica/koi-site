Why it exists

Five apps and a notes file, and none of them knew about each other.

5apps
+ 1 notes file
GoalsHabitsWorkoutsMealsTo-dosNotes

Goals in one place. Habits in another. Workouts somewhere else. Each app was fine on its own.

The problem was that no single one of them could answer the only question that matters: am I actually doing the things I said I would?

To-do apps are too rigid and only hold tasks. Notion and its relatives are too open-ended, so you spend more time building the system than using it. Koi is opinionated. It has a point of view about what you need to see, so there is nothing to assemble before you start.

Works with what you use

Keep your tools. Koi keeps you on track.

Almost everyone we talked to had tried a system before. Those systems rarely failed loudly. They died quietly, because keeping them up took effort and nothing held anyone to it. So Koi doesn't ask you to move your life into a new app. It writes to the places you already check, and spends its effort on accountability.

How it's built

Anyone can add a screen. Most of the work has been removal.

Opinionated

Every feature makes the product slightly less clear about what it's for. Koi decides what you need to see so you don't have to build it.

Shaped by interviews

Notes integration, personalized notifications, and widgets all came from people describing how their own systems fell apart.

Built for resets

Falling off is normal. Koi is designed around getting back on track, not around a perfect streak.
